SPHERICAL BACKGROUND RESOLUTION EXPERIMENT - RESULTS SO FAR
============================================================

Date written: 2026-08-07
Result folder: C:\Coding\CGProject\nag1\runs\2026-08-05_sphvisuv_dubres_regular


1. PURPOSE
----------

Test whether allocating the normal sphere-texture budget only to the UV area
seen by the cameras ("visible-UV") can improve spherical-background quality
without the memory cost of a globally double-resolution sphere texture.

All experiments use static Waymo final scenes, a view-dependent spherical
background, and the sphere radius computed from visible plane centers.


2. VARIANTS
-----------

Regular sphere
  - color_mask_resolution: 960 x 1920
  - Standard full-sphere texture mapping.

Double-resolution sphere ("dubres")
  - color_mask_resolution: 1920 x 3840
  - Standard full-sphere texture mapping.

Visible-UV sphere
  - color_mask_resolution: 960 x 1920 (the normal-resolution texel budget).
  - The whole budget is packed into a UV tile covering the camera-visible
    portion of the sphere.
  - margin_uv: [0.05, 0.05]
  - interior_samples_per_axis: 9
  - Outside the tile, the background uses a fallback colour.

Visible-UV run 1 is the initial run set. Visible-UV run 2 repeats exactly the
same six visible-UV configurations after fallback-lookup instrumentation was
fixed and committed.


3. UV TILE DENSITY AND FALLBACK SAFETY
--------------------------------------

Visible-UV tile coverage/density:
  W975 175-195: 22.02% of sphere UV area,  4.54x local texel density
  W125 124-149:  6.98% of sphere UV area, 14.34x local texel density
  W141 174-197:  7.25% of sphere UV area, 13.80x local texel density
  W203   3-58 :  7.05% of sphere UV area, 14.19x local texel density
  W344  95-135:  7.17% of sphere UV area, 13.94x local texel density
  W324 161-197:  7.20% of sphere UV area, 13.89x local texel density

Visible-UV run 2 logged the actual texture lookup coordinates after learned
flow and sphere UV wrapping/clamping. Results:

  fallback_uv_fraction: 0 for every training step and every epoch
  fallback_uv_count:    0 for every training epoch
  Total observed training-time background RGB lookups: 3.852 billion

Therefore, learned flow did not leave the local tile in any of these six
static-scene training runs. The current 0.05 UV margin is sufficient for these
data and camera trajectories. This is evidence for training-time lookups; it
does not automatically prove the same margin is safe for entirely new camera
trajectories or a different scene.

6. INTERPRETATION OF QUALITY RESULTS
------------------------------------

Visible-UV is technically successful: it concentrates 4.5x to 14.3x more
texel density on the used sphere region, never used fallback colour during
training, and has no visible seam/artifact in inspected renders.

The average visible-UV metrics are slightly better than both baselines.
However, the improvement is too small to call conclusive because training is
not deterministic. The two otherwise identical visible-UV runs differ by up
to 0.0487 dB MeanMPSNR on an individual scene. That repeat-to-repeat variation
is larger than most visible-UV versus baseline differences.

Accordingly:
  - Visible-UV is at least competitive with global double resolution while
    retaining the normal-resolution texture budget.
  - The present runs do NOT yet prove a reliable, visually material quality
    improvement over regular or double-resolution sphere textures.
  - The initial apparent advantage over double resolution should be treated as
    promising, not established, until controlled seed repeats are available.

The final eval_epoch Vehicle/Human metrics are semantic object-group metrics,
not background-only metrics. In visible-UV run 2, regular sphere has higher
Vehicle MPSNR/SSIM in five of six scenes. This is an important caution against
claiming a broad general-quality win from the current data.

8. RECOMMENDED NEXT EXPERIMENT
------------------------------

For a statistically useful decision:

  1. Select two representative scenes (for example W125 and W324).
  2. Run regular, double-resolution, and visible-UV variants with 2-3 fixed
     seeds each.
  3. Compare mean and spread for MeanMPSNR, MeanSSIM, MeanLPIPS, and relevant
     final eval_epoch metrics.
  4. Add a background-specific metric or stable image crops (sky, building,
     road) if the primary question is background detail; current Vehicle/Human
     metrics are indirect measures of background quality.

The current configs have deterministic: false, so single runs cannot reliably
resolve effects on the order of approximately 0.01 to 0.05 dB.
